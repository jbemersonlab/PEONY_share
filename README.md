# PEONY
A global reference database of DNA viral (vOTU) sequences from viral size-fractionated metagenomes (viromes) 

 Here we contribute to publicly available phage databases by updating PIGEON (Phages, and Integrated Genomes Encapsidated or Not), which is among the largest dsDNA viromics-specific databases to date. We reintroduce PIGEON as PEONY (Phages Encapsidated ONlY.) to reflect the increasing availalility of viral size-fractionated metagenomic (viromics) data.

PIGEON1.0 was first assembled and made publicly available in 2021 with 266,125 viral operational taxonomic units (vOTUs > 10 kbp, clustered at 95% average nucleotide identity, ANI) from peat, soil, freshwater, marine, mammalian digestive, and plant-associated environments. The database was then updated in 2023 (PIGEON2.0)8 to include additional vOTUs totaling 515,763. Due to the limited numbers of sequenced viromes at the time, particularly from soil, PIGEON1.0 and 2.0 included vOTUs recovered both from viromes and from total DNA metagenomes. PIGEON3.0 increased the total number of dereplicated vOTUs to 1,147,011. After removing 263,711 vOTUs from total metagenomes in PIGEON2.0 and reclustering with new the data, 945,661 vOTUs remained in PEONY. 

To complete the present update, we compiled new virome datasets from our own recently published and as-yet unpublished work and from a Web of Science literature search. 
PEONY vOTUs encompass terrestrial, aquatic, and mammalian digestive ecosystems across five continents. Notably, 65% of vOTUs in PEONY originate from California, USA, where soils from various ecosystems (agricultural fields, grasslands, forests, wetlands, chaparral, and plant rhizospheres) have been routinely sampled by the Emerson Lab at the University of California, Davis and collaborators in recent years. This database provides a useful tool for studies of dsDNA bacteriophage diversity and environmental distributions. Researchers can use PEONY to compare viruses from individual studies to global distribution patterns. For example, mapping vOTUs from fire-affected soils to the PIGEON2.0 database allowed Geonczy et al.12 to identify potentially fire-adapted vOTUs shared amongst other post-wildfire studies. As more viromics data becomes available we expect to update PEONY biennially (funding-dependent).

The databases (both PIGEON3.0 and PEONY) and associated metadata are available on Dryad ([https://datadryad.org, DOI: 10.5061/dryad.prr4xgz35](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.prr4xgz35)). Scripts for database curation and visualization, and supplemental information regarding study inclusion criteria are included here.
